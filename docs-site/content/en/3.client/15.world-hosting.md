With the TRS Client you can open your singleplayer world for your TRS friends – no port forwarding, no server. Friends connect directly when possible and through the TRS relay otherwise.

::note
World hosting needs the TRS online features and works from Minecraft 1.8.9 on – not on 1.7.10, 1.13.2 and Forge 1.14.4.
::

## Host your world

::steps
### Open the pause menu
Start your singleplayer world and press :kbd{value="Esc"}. Click **Host world** at the bottom left.

### Choose the settings
**World name**, **Game mode** (Survival, Creative, Adventure), **Cheats (for everyone)**, **PvP**, **Max players** (up to 10) and **Who can see it** – **Friends** or **Invited players only**. **Back up the world first** is recommended.

### Click Host
The world is backed up and opened. You get a **join code** like `K7Q-M2X` to copy and share.
::

![The Host world window with players and join code](/shots/client/hosting.png)

## Manage your world

Open **Manage hosting** in the pause menu. The window has four tabs:

- **Players** – every player with their connection (**Direct** or **Via relay**) and ping. Give them **OP**, make them **Spectator** or take away **Build**; remove or ban them – optionally for all your worlds.
- **Requests** – accept or decline players who asked to join (also right from a notification).
- **Invite** – invite friends; online friends are listed first.
- **Settings** – change the settings while the world is open.

**Stop hosting** closes the world for everyone.

::tip
**Direct connections (world hosting)** in the TRS menu under **Social** is on by default. Direct connections show your IP address to the other player – turn it off to use only the TRS relay.
::

## Join a friend's world

- **Invite:** an invite pops up as a notification – press :kbd{value="Y"} or click **Join**.
- **Social → Worlds:** lists your friends' open worlds with **Join** or **Request**.
- **World cards in chat** have a **Join** button.
- **Join with code:** the multiplayer screen has a **Join with code** button.

If the host plays another Minecraft version, you see that clearly before you try. From the TRS Launcher you can also join through **Social → Worlds** in the launcher – it picks or creates a matching instance and connects by itself.

## Share mods and a resource pack

When hosting, you can share your mods and a resource pack with your guests. Both are off by default and remembered per world.

- **Share mods** – pick which mods go along and whether each is required or optional. Mods that add blocks or items are preselected as required, pure client mods are not selected. Mods are recognised on Modrinth and, with the TRS Launcher, on CurseForge.
- **Send mods directly from the host** – mods that are in no store are sent straight from you; your guests see a warning. Off: those mods only show up as "get it yourself".
- **Share resource pack** – the pack goes straight from you to your guests (never through TRS), up to 250 MB. They get Minecraft's usual question whether to use it. From Minecraft 1.20.3 on.

Guests who miss required mods can open the world in the TRS Launcher with **Open in launcher** – it sets up a matching instance for them.

## Public link (optional)

For friends without the TRS Client you can turn on a **Public link** through the e4mc service. It is off by default and needs a confirmed warning every single time.

::warning
Anyone with the link can join your world. e4mc is a separate third-party service that sees the IP addresses of everyone who connects – the TRS services are not involved. Only share the link with people you trust, and turn it off with **Deactivate** when you are done.
::

While the link is on, a small hint in the pause menu reminds you of it.
